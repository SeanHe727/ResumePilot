# Action verbs & voice

Harvard FAS Mignone Center, verbatim: resume language must be **"active rather than passive"**,
**"specific rather than general"**, and **"written to express not impress"**. Personal pronouns and
narrative prose are explicitly forbidden.

Third on its list of top mistakes: **"using passive language instead of 'action' words"**.

---

### Q: Why are "responsible for" and "worked on" a problem

> Source: FAANG resume conventions — bystander language

**Weak**: "Responsible for developing the user centre module; participated in architecture
discussions."

**Strong**:

"Rebuilt the user-centre auth flow, collapsing login from 4 serial RPCs to 1, and drove the
resulting design into all 6 business lines."

- "Responsible for" becomes "rebuilt", "collapsing", "drove" — three things *you did*
- "Participated in discussions" becomes "drove into 6 business lines" — from attendee to owner

**Gap**: "Responsible for" and "worked on" are **bystander language**: they describe the slot you
were assigned, not the action you took. A reader cannot tell whether you designed the thing, wrote
it, or sat in the meeting. FAANG hiring conventions call these out explicitly as phrases to strip.

### Q: Does starting every bullet with a verb feel mechanical

> Source: Harvard FAS — begin each line with an action verb; phrases, not sentences

**Weak**: "In this project, I was mainly doing the work of building the data pipeline, and then I
also did some monitoring."

**Strong**:

"Built an Airflow pipeline scheduling 40+ daily jobs with 12 SLA alerts, cutting data-delay
tickets from 5 per week to 0.5."

- Verb-first, no pronoun, not a full sentence
- "And then I also did some monitoring" compressed into a second action with a number attached

**Gap**: Harvard's rule is that each line is a **phrase, not a sentence** — "Led team of 8", not
"I led a team of 8". That is not pedantry, it is **information density**: "In this project, I was
mainly doing the work of" carries zero information while consuming one of the six seconds you get.
A resume is scanned, not read.

### Q: Are some verbs genuinely stronger than others

> Source: Harvard action verb lists / FAANG bar-raiser patterns

**Weak**: "Helped the team handle production issues and assisted with release."

**Strong**:

"Diagnosed and fixed a connection-pool leak causing 200 daily 5xx errors; ran the v2.3 canary
from 15% traffic to full rollout."

- "Helped handle" becomes "diagnosed and fixed" — the specific action is now visible
- "Assisted with" becomes "ran the canary" — your level of ownership is now visible

**Gap**: Verb strength is not about vocabulary. The test is whether **the verb reconstructs a
concrete action**. "Helped", "assisted", "supported", "contributed to" reconstruct nothing — the
reader cannot tell whether you wrote code, ran a meeting, or fetched coffee. "Diagnosed", "fixed",
"rebuilt", "migrated", "led" each name something an interviewer can ask a follow-up about.
**That is the standard: could someone ask "how, specifically?" about this verb.**

### Q: Do these rules apply to a resume written in Chinese

> Source: the language-independent half of the Harvard FAS rules

**Weak**: "During my internship I participated in the development of the company's core system and
gained rich experience."

**Strong**:

"Rewrote the order state machine as 12 explicit states, replacing 40+ scattered if-else
branches; state-related production defects fell from 6 per month to 0."

- Pronoun gone, self-assessment ("gained rich experience") gone
- "Participated in development" becomes "rewrote the order state machine"

**Gap**: No pronouns, no self-assessment, verb-first — all three are **language-independent** and
apply equally to a Chinese resume. "Gained rich experience" is textbook self-evaluation; Harvard's
phrasing is "written to express not impress", meaning the reader should conclude your experience is
rich from your facts, not from your adjective. Chinese resumes carry one extra trap: **stacked
particles** (「负责核心业务系统的性能优化的相关工作」) — deleting them costs no information and
saves a third of the length.
