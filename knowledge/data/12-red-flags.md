# Red flags

Harvard FAS lists five top mistakes: **spelling and grammar errors, missing email and phone,
passive language instead of action words, poor organisation that resists skimming, and failing to
demonstrate results.**

This dimension covers the ones that are disqualifying — not "could be better" but "must be fixed".

---

### Q: What is the single most damaging mistake

> Source: Harvard FAS — top mistakes

**Weak**: A beautiful resume with the email in the page header, or the phone number simply
missing.

**Strong**:

First line under the name: email · phone · city · GitHub/LinkedIn — **in the body, never in a
header or footer**.

**Gap**: Harvard lists "missing email and phone information" among five top mistakes, which sounds
too basic to be real — but it happens constantly, because most people **did include it, in the
wrong place**. Headers and footers are the region parsers most often discard (~22% of errors). If
nobody can reach you, everything else was wasted.

---

### Q: Should I explain a gap in the timeline

> Source: Harvard FAS — "avoid information gaps such as a missing summer"

**Weak**: Nothing at all between 2023.06 and 2024.03.

**Strong**:

"2023.06 – 2024.03  Self-directed study and open source: completed all 4 projects of CMU 15-445;
submitted 7 PRs to project X (all merged)"

- The gap is filled with something **checkable**
- Exam preparation, caring for family, a failed startup can each be stated plainly in one line
  without dressing up

**Gap**: Harvard explicitly asks you to avoid information gaps. A blank does not get skipped —
**the reader fills it in, and usually worse than the truth.** One line of explanation costs almost
nothing and removes an invisible negative assumption.

### Q: Where does exaggeration become fabrication

> Source: how technical interviews verify claims

**Weak**: "Expert in distributed systems"; "solo-architected a system serving 10M users" (in
reality one module, in a team of three).

**Strong**:

"Owned the recall layer in a 5-person recommender rebuild" — scope, role and module all true.

**Gap**: Exaggeration in technical hiring has a peculiar property: **it is almost certain to be
verified on the spot.** "Expert" invites unlimited depth; "solo-architected" invites questions about
design trade-offs. Whatever the resume gained is returned ten minutes into the interview, with a
credibility penalty attached. **Being able to state the boundary of your own contribution is itself
a seniority signal** — the same point the scope dimension makes.

---

### Q: What other low-level but frequent mistakes are there

> Source: Harvard FAS — formatting and content prohibitions

**Weak**: Photo, age and gender, a references list, every line starting with a date, first-person
narrative.

**Strong**:

Harvard's prohibition list:
- No personal pronouns (I / we)
- No abbreviations, no narrative style, no slang
- No photo, no age or gender
- No references list (they will be requested separately)
- **Do not start each line with a date**
- Check the formatting survives conversion to PDF

**Gap**: These are **rule mistakes** — they need no judgement, only knowledge. The date-first rule
is the most commonly overlooked: leading with a date puts the reader's eye on the timeline instead
of the achievement, and makes it easy for a parser to misread the whole line as a date field.
