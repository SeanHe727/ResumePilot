# Education & early career

The core difficulty for students and early-career candidates: **the experience itself carries less
weight, so differences in how it is written matter more.**

Harvard's rules matter especially here, because student resumes most often make two mistakes —
writing duties instead of results, and writing process instead of conclusions.

---

### Q: Where should education go, and how much of it

> Source: ATS section-order conventions / Harvard's reverse-chronological rule

**Weak**: Education last, reading only "University X, Computer Science, BSc".

**Strong**:

Students and new graduates: education directly after contact details. Three or more years of
work: after the experience section.

"Tongji University, BSc Computer Science | 2021 – 2025
GPA 3.8/4.0 (top 5%); relevant coursework: Distributed Systems, Compilers, Database Internals"

- Within a section, **reverse chronological** — most recent first
- Include GPA if it helps, omit it otherwise; list only coursework relevant to the target role

**Gap**: For a new graduate the school and the grades are often the strongest card, and putting
them last buries it. Conversely, education still leading after five years of work suggests the work
cannot carry the page. **Position itself communicates "this is what I most want you to see".**

---

### Q: Do course projects count as project experience

> Source: Harvard — early experience is held to the same outcome standard

**Weak**: "Operating systems course project: implemented a simple file system."

**Strong**:

"Implemented a FAT32-style file system (2,400 lines of C) with concurrent read/write and crash
consistency; passed 38 of the course's 38 tests and was the only submission in the class to
implement journal recovery."

- Code volume, tests passed, scarcity (only one)
- The self-deprecating "simple" is deleted outright

**Gap**: Course projects absolutely count — but **they have to be written to the standard of real
projects**. In "implemented a simple file system", "simple" is self-deprecation and "implemented"
has no result attached. The commonest student mistake is **doing the reader's subtraction for
them**: say it does not matter and the reader will agree.

---

### Q: What if I have no internships

> Source: Harvard FAS — "avoid information gaps"

**Weak**: Education only, everything else left blank.

**Strong**:

Open source, competitions, teaching assistance, technical roles in societies, personal projects
— written to the same XYZ standard.

"Submitted 7 PRs to open-source project X (all merged); the logging-module refactor cut memory
use by 30%."

- Merged count is **externally verifiable**
- Competitions get placing and field size; TA work gets student numbers and the course

**Gap**: Harvard explicitly asks you to avoid information gaps. A blank does not make the reader
skip it — **it makes them fill it in themselves, usually worse than the truth.** Externally
verifiable experience (open source, competitions) carries unusual weight for new graduates
precisely because it does not depend on a company's endorsement.
