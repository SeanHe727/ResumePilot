# Resume Review

## Overall assessment

Your resume points most strongly toward **product management, product operations, or operations strategy roles**, with an MBA, product internship, logistics analytics background, and product-led projects.

The strongest evidence is:

- Measurable product outcomes at Harbor Payments
- Cross-functional operating experience at Crestline Logistics
- User research and growth metrics from the food-rescue app
- An MBA in progress/completed
- Experience improving processes at meaningful scale

The main problem is not lack of experience. It is **positioning and prioritization**. The resume currently makes the reader work too hard to understand whether you are:

1. A product manager,
2. An operations leader moving into product, or
3. A generalist MBA candidate with several unrelated experiences.

You should make the product/operations strategy direction explicit and reduce duplication, especially in the Harbor Payments section.

## 4. Add a summary only if it is specific

### What to change

A short summary would help if it clearly connects:

- Economics and MBA training
- Operations and analytics experience
- Product experimentation and user research
- Your target role

Do not add a generic objective or a list of adjectives.

### Why

Your background requires explanation. A targeted summary can prevent the recruiter from treating the roles as unrelated. Without a summary, the experience section must perform all of that narrative work.

## 5. Resolve metric precision

### What to change

Audit every number for:

- Baseline and endpoint
- Time period
- Percentage versus percentage-point change
- Modeled versus realized financial impact
- Pilot versus company-wide scope
- Your role in producing the result
- Whether the result is causal or merely associated with the change

The most important items to check are:

- “5-point conversion lift”
- “150% increase”
- “20% ... from 75% to 95%”
- “$2.4M annual saving”
- “1,800 driver hours”
- “9 tonnes ... would have been thrown away”

### Why

Your resume is metric-rich, which is a major strength. But metrics also receive more scrutiny. Precision problems can undermine otherwise excellent achievements.

**Likely reaction:** Interested, but likely to probe ownership.
