## Overall assessment

This is already a strong quantitative-research resume: it is technical, results-oriented, and unusually well quantified. The main improvements are to tighten potentially overstated claims, make research outputs easier to verify, resolve role/date ambiguity, and strengthen the skills section.

## Contact information

- **Name:** Keep as is.
- **Phone and email:** Keep, assuming these are placeholders for your real details.
- **Code-profile URL:** Label the destination or use a recognizable domain. Recruiters should immediately know whether it is GitHub, a personal site, or a portfolio.
- **Missing links:** Add LinkedIn only if it is complete and consistent with the resume. Add links to the package, working paper, or publications where appropriate rather than making readers search for them.

## Formatting and structure

- Use one consistent date format and one dash style throughout.
- Keep bullets to roughly one or two lines where possible; several currently wrap to three lines.
- Consider limiting Northpeak to four or five bullets and the research role to three or four. The strongest Northpeak bullets are the signal result, cost reduction, validation, feature infrastructure, and live-allocation decision.
- Add a **Research/Publications** section if the JASA manuscript and volatility paper are both substantive and public. That would make your academic record easier to assess.
- Maintain reverse chronological order.
- Keep the resume to one page for most internship or early-career quant applications unless publications require a second page.
- Ensure every numerical claim is explainable, reproducible, and permissible to disclose. Quant interviewers are likely to probe the Sharpe, turnover, slippage, significance tests, leakage controls, and tighter-bound claims closely.
