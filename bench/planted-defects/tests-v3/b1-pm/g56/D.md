# Resume Review

**Target role inferred from your resume:** Associate Product Manager / Product Manager  
**Overall assessment:** Strong foundation with good metrics, cross-functional experience, and a credible transition from operations into product. However, the resume currently has several credibility and coherence problems—especially one clearly duplicated or misplaced bullet, a potentially misleading metric, inconsistent tense, and an overly detailed personal-information section.

## Highest-priority changes

1. **Remove or correct the “new merchant” bullet under the hospital consulting practicum.**
   - The final bullet under **MBA Consulting Practicum** is nearly identical to the Harbor Payments onboarding bullet.
   - It refers to merchants, even though the project is for a regional hospital network.
   - This is the most serious issue because it can look like copied content, fabrication, or careless editing.
   - Delete it unless it genuinely belongs to the Harbor Payments role. If it belongs to Harbor Payments, place it there and remove it from the practicum.

2. **Remove your date of birth and nationality.**
   - They are not relevant to product hiring and can create avoidable bias or compliance concerns.
   - If work authorization is important, state that separately only when required and in the appropriate form.
   - Keep your phone, email, LinkedIn, and portfolio only if those links are real and current.

3. **Fix the late-delivery metric.**
   - The bullet says late deliveries fell from 11% to 7% by excluding weather-delayed shipments from the calculation.
   - That does not clearly demonstrate an operational improvement; it changes the measurement method.
   - A hiring manager may interpret this as manipulating the metric rather than improving performance.
   - Either replace the bullet with a genuinely improved outcome or clearly explain the analytical/reporting purpose of the change. Do not present a denominator or definition change as a performance gain.

4. **Clarify the scope and causality of the Harbor Payments metrics.**
   - “Improved checkout conversion by 35%” is very strong, but the bullet does not specify the time period, sample size, test design, or whether the result was measured through an experiment.
   - “For all merchants” also sounds unusually broad for a summer intern project.
   - Make the measurement basis and scope precise. Avoid implying that the redesign caused the entire improvement if the result came from a simple before-and-after comparison.

5. **Resolve the overlap between your two onboarding bullets.**
   - The Harbor Payments section contains both:
     - the onboarding redesign that reduced time to first payment from 9 days to 4, and
     - the measurement bullet comparing merchants who opted into the new flow with those who stayed on the old one.
   - These may describe the same initiative, but the relationship is not clear.
   - Make the sequence explicit: problem, intervention, evaluation method, and result. If the comparison was not randomized, avoid presenting it as definitive causal evidence.

6. **Correct grammar and tense.**
   - The S&OP bullet uses present tense—“prepares”—while the role is in the past.
   - “Analysed” is British spelling while the resume otherwise uses American context and terminology. Use one spelling convention consistently.
   - Check all bullets for consistent past tense, especially because the resume includes both completed and ongoing projects.

## Bottom line

Your resume has stronger evidence than many early-career APM resumes, particularly in its use of measurable outcomes. The main problem is not a lack of accomplishments; it is that several accomplishments are currently difficult to trust or interpret. Fix the duplicated bullet, remove irrelevant personal information, correct the misleading logistics metric, clarify measurement methods, and strengthen the product narrative. Those changes should materially improve both recruiter confidence and hiring-manager interest.
