Your strongest material is the trial coordination and data-quality work: it has scale, methods, and measurable outcomes. I’d fix the statistical and study-design claims first, because they could undermine confidence in the rest of the resume. I’ll describe changes rather than rewrite your lines.

### Header and education
- **Date of birth and nationality:** Remove these for most U.S. applications; they generally aren’t needed on a resume. Include work-authorization information only if an application asks for it.
- **Education:** Add your undergraduate degree if you have one. Keep the M.P.H. as listed.

### Meridian Heart Institute
- **Three-site trial:** Clarify whether 640 is the number enrolled and what the 94% follow-up figure measures. Those details make the strong results easier to interpret.
- **Data-entry errors:** State how the error rates were measured, if you can do so briefly. This supports the before-and-after claim.
- **Median survival:** **Correct or remove this bullet.** Averaging follow-up times only among patients who died does not estimate median survival; excluding censored patients can bias the result. Describe the analysis you actually performed, without calling it median survival unless you used an appropriate survival-analysis method.
- **Adverse-event reconciliation:** Make the verb tense consistent with the other bullets. Clarify whether the drop from 45 to 6 reflects your reconciliation process and over what period.
