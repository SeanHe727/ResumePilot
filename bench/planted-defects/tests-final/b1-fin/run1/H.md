## Formatting and content-wide changes

- **Use past tense for completed roles and projects.** The only present-tense wording that stands out is “Writes,” but review every verb.
- **Remove first-person language.** The phrase “my findings” is unnecessary in a resume bullet.
- **Standardize date formatting.** Use one month format and one dash style everywhere.
- **Remove line-break hyphenation artifacts.** Words such as “fixed-rate” should not be split across lines by manual hyphens. Let the document wrap naturally.
- **Prioritize outcomes.** Several bullets describe an activity but not its consequence, especially the capital-plan, acquisition-valuation, and onboarding bullets.
- **Clarify causality.** Statements such as treasury paying down debt because of your forecast should be accurate and defensible.
- **Use consistent units.** You use “USD 600M,” “USD 40M,” and “USD 50.” Keep the currency format consistent and specify whether amounts refer to company revenue, debt, share price, or another measure.
- **Check every percentage.** Your 25% sensitivity figure is incorrect, and the “12%” forecast-accuracy improvement needs more precise labeling.
- **Tailor the resume to the target role.** For credit roles, emphasize underwriting, covenant monitoring, portfolio risk, and approval outcomes. For corporate finance roles, emphasize forecasting, close acceleration, capital planning, and process automation. For valuation or investment roles, emphasize technically correct valuation methodology and investment conclusions.
- **Consider whether the student project still earns its space.** Your professional experience is now strong enough that older academic experience should be retained only if it supports your target role directly.
