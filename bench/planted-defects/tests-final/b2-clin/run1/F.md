# Resume review

You have a strong base: the resume includes trial scale, measurable outcomes, and relevant research experience. The biggest priorities are correcting two statistical claims, removing personal information that is not useful for hiring, and making the project results easier to assess.

## Header

- **Name, phone, email, and LinkedIn:** Keep these. Make sure the LinkedIn URL is complete and clickable in the submitted PDF.
- **Date of birth:** Remove it. It is not needed to evaluate your qualifications and may invite age-related bias.
- **Nationality:** Remove it unless a specific application asks for it or it is relevant to work authorization. If work authorization is relevant, state that directly and accurately instead.
- **Location:** Add a city/region if useful for the roles you’re targeting; a full street address is unnecessary.

## Education

- **M.P.H. in Biostatistics:** Keep the degree and dates. Consider placing Education after Experience, since you have several years of relevant work history.
- **Missing undergraduate education:** Add it if it is relevant to the roles you’re pursuing or helps complete your education history. If you omit it, make sure the omission is intentional and not confusing.
- **Location and date formatting:** Use a consistent format throughout the resume. The current mix of city/state and month/year dates is understandable, but consistency improves scanning.

## Experience

### Meridian Heart Institute | Clinical Research Coordinator

- **Three-site trial bullet:** Keep this; it is one of your strongest bullets. If available, clarify your specific contribution to the trial and what “94% of follow-up visits completed” means (for example, the denominator or measurement period). This helps readers interpret the metric.
- **Data-entry error bullet:** Keep the before-and-after rates. Clarify how the error rate was measured, if you can do so briefly and accurately; otherwise, be prepared to explain it in an interview.
- **Survival-analysis bullet:** **Revisit or remove this claim.** Averaging follow-up time among patients who died and excluding censored patients is not a valid way to estimate median survival. Survival estimates need to account for censoring, typically using a method such as Kaplan–Meier. Only describe an analysis you actually conducted and can defend; if the method was different, make sure the resume accurately reflects it.
- **Adverse-event bullet:** Change the verb tense so it matches the rest of this past role. The bullet also has a manual line break splitting a word; remove manual breaks and let the document wrap naturally. Keep the reduction from 45 to 6, but be ready to explain the time period and what counted as an unresolved query.
- **Safety-report bullet:** Consider whether “all 4 reviews passed without a protocol change” demonstrates your contribution or could be misread as claiming that no protocol changes were needed because of your work. Be precise about your role and what the review outcome actually signifies.
- **Training/audit bullet:** Keep the training and audit detail, but clarify the scope of the audit if “no consent deviations” applied only to the records reviewed rather than the entire study.

### Northgate University Hospital | Research Assistant

- **Odd/even-day assignment bullet:** **Do not describe this as randomized allocation.** Assigning participants based on admission day is predictable and can introduce selection bias; it is not equivalent to randomization. If this accurately describes the study, characterize the design and allocation method precisely, and don’t imply stronger causal evidence than it supports.
- **Missed-visit interviews bullet:** This is useful because it connects research to an operational outcome. Clarify whether the reduction from 18% to 11% was measured after the reminders were introduced and over what period, if known.
- **Data dictionary bullet:** Keep this. Reuse by two later studies is a concrete indication of lasting value. If you know the data dictionary’s role in enabling those studies, make that contribution clear.
- **Data-cleaning and administrative duties bullet:** The bullet combines several unrelated duties, which makes the identifier-resolution result hard to connect to your work. Separate or prioritize the data-cleaning achievement, and trim routine administrative tasks unless they are important for the target role. Clarify what “resolved 1,200 mismatched patient identifiers” means and how you contributed to resolving them.

## Projects

### Asthma Readmission Analysis

- **“Harnessed cutting-edge analytics…” bullet:** Remove it. It is vague and promotional, and it doesn’t tell a reviewer what you analyzed, how you analyzed it, or what you found.
- **Readmission result bullet:** **Correct the percentage interpretation.** A change from 12% to 8% is a decrease of 4 percentage points, or about a 33% relative reduction—not a 50% reduction. Also, “cut” implies a causal effect. Unless the project design supports a causal conclusion, describe the result as an association or observed difference. Include the sample size and uncertainty measures, such as confidence intervals, if available.
- **Adjustment for age and insurance:** This alone may not address confounding. Be prepared to explain the analysis design, comparison groups, model, and other important limitations. State only what the analysis actually supports.
- **Survey response bullet:** The change from 35% to 55% is a 20-percentage-point increase, not a 20% increase. Also clarify whether this is the same survey or a separate outcome from the readmission analysis; the current project description makes their relationship unclear.
- **Project scope and dates:** Since the project is ongoing, identify what is complete and what remains only if that distinction matters to the role. Make sure the project is clearly labeled as independent and that the underlying data and findings are accurately represented.

### Community Health Survey

- **Survey design and responses bullet:** Keep this. It gives scale and a practical outcome. Clarify whether the 1,100 responses were complete responses, and whether the neighborhood estimates were based on a representative sample or should be interpreted cautiously.
- **Translation and pilot bullet:** Keep if translation and piloting were substantive parts of your contribution. The sample of 20 is useful context, but avoid implying it establishes broad validity.
- **Codebook bullet:** Keep; it shows that your work was reused. Specify your individual contribution if “wrote” could overstate a team effort.

## Skills

- **Tools:** R and REDCap are relevant, but the section may be too sparse for biostatistics roles. Add other tools you can use confidently and discuss in an interview—potentially statistical software, SQL, spreadsheets, or clinical data systems, if applicable.
- **Methods:** Include only methods you have actually applied. Consider naming specific analyses or study-design methods that match your target roles, but don’t list methods based only on coursework or brief exposure.
- **Organization:** Group tools and methods consistently, and consider adding relevant clinical-research competencies or certifications if you have them. Avoid proficiency ratings unless an application specifically requests them.

## Overall presentation and positioning

- **Section order:** For research or biostatistics roles, consider putting Experience and Skills before Education, given your relevant work history.
- **Target role:** The resume could be tailored differently for biostatistician, clinical research, and data-analysis positions. Prioritize the most relevant methods and achievements for each application.
- **Formatting:** Remove manual line breaks inside bullets and check that dates, punctuation, and bullet formatting are consistent. Keep the document easy to scan and ATS-readable.
- **Most urgent fixes:** Correct the survival-analysis description, stop calling odd/even-day allocation randomized, correct the readmission reduction calculation, and review causal wording across the project bullets.
