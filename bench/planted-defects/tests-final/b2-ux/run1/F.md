## Experience

### Pinegrove Financial — Senior UX Researcher

1. **Card-dispute A/B test:** Keep this prominent; it has a clear outcome and timeframe. Add the sample size or test volume and clarify how the abandonment rate was measured, if you have those figures. If the result was not statistically significant, don’t imply the test established a definitive effect.

2. **Accessibility / design system:** Correct the contrast claim before using it. WCAG AA generally requires a **4.5:1 contrast ratio for normal text** and **3:1 for large text**; 3:1 is not the general body-text threshold. Also, meeting a contrast threshold alone does not establish that a design system conforms to WCAG 2.1 AA. Narrow the claim to what you actually audited or changed, and only claim broader conformance if it was assessed across the applicable criteria.

3. **Research synthesis and support calls:** Fix the tense: this is a completed role, but the bullet begins with a present-participle form. Also, the sentence currently makes the reduction in repeat calls sound like a direct result of synthesizing research. Clarify what changed based on the findings and how the 23% reduction was measured. If the analysis covered 18 months of tickets, specify the relevant before-and-after period for the outcome.

4. **Presentations and shipped fixes:** This is useful evidence that your research influenced product decisions. Clarify what “the top five” refers to and whether the three fixes shipped in the same release cycle. If possible, indicate the scope or result of those shipped fixes rather than relying only on the number adopted.

5. **PM training:** Strong evidence of enabling others and increasing research capacity. Clarify the comparison period behind “doubled” and, if you can support it, whether the additional tests were completed or used in decisions—not just initiated.

6. **Mobile onboarding:** Strong outcome and clear connection to launch. Clarify the measurement window and, if available, the number of customers or sign-ups behind the 41% and 58% figures. Make sure “completed sign-ups” is measured consistently before and after launch.

### Northwind Health — UX Research Associate

1. **NPS:** This is the most important metric issue in the experience section. NPS is not the average of 0–10 recommendation ratings; it is calculated from the proportions of promoters and detractors. A rise from 6.1 to 8.4 sounds like an average rating, not an NPS result. Verify the metric and label it accurately before submitting. Also clarify the comparison period and avoid implying the research alone caused the change unless you can support that attribution.

2. **Appointment reminders:** Clear research-to-product-to-outcome chain. If available, add the time period or appointment volume used to calculate the missed-appointment rates. Keep the wording proportionate to the evidence for the reminder’s effect.

3. **Study templates:** Change the present tense to match the completed role. The reported setup-time reduction is strong; clarify whether those times are typical or averages, if known.

4. **Telehealth waiting room:** Good, specific audience and outcome. Add the measurement period or call-volume basis for the 30% reduction if available. “Older patients” is understandable, but a defined age range is more precise if that was part of the study criteria.
