## Detailed review: what to change and why

### Header and education

- **Name and contact details:** These are clear. Add a target-role signal near the top, because a reader should not have to infer whether you’re pursuing product work.
- **Education order and dates:** The MBA is correctly first and its dates are clear. The overlap between the MBA and your 2024 internship and projects is ordinary and does not need explanation on its own.
- **B.A. entry:** It is clear and complete. Consider whether both locations add value; they use space without helping establish your PM fit.

### Coastal Marine Services

- **Engine and gearbox rebuild bullet:** This gives a clear scope—14 vessels—and a turnaround measure. For a PM résumé, explain the relevance through the kinds of operational constraints, coordination, or reliability outcomes you handled; otherwise, a reader may see it as a sharp departure from your product experience.
- **Hydraulic winch and inspection-record bullet:** This reads as routine maintenance and recordkeeping. Add a meaningful outcome if you have one, such as inspection results, reduced downtime, safety, or reliability; otherwise, consider whether this is the best use of space for a PM application.

### Crestline Logistics

- **Carrier-scorecard bullet:** “Owned” and “discussed them” establish responsibility, but not what changed because of your work. Show the scorecards’ scale, a decision they informed, or an improvement they produced—or give this space to a stronger result.
- **Pick-error reduction bullet:** This is one of your stronger operations examples: it has a measured result, two sites, supervisor collaboration, and training at scale. Clarify the measurement period and, if known, the business consequence of fewer errors.
- **S&OP and forecast-error bullet:** Strong evidence of recurring cross-functional ownership and a measurable result. Clarify what the forecast-error measure represents and whether the reduction held over the 12 quarters or was the final observed result.
- **Route-planning rollout bullet:** The 1,800-hour annual saving is compelling, but this bullet combines rollout, SOP changes, driver training, and contract negotiations. Separate the main achievement from secondary responsibilities or reduce the activity list so the measured impact is easier to absorb. Clarify how the hours saved were estimated.

### Harbor Payments

- **Merchant-onboarding bullet:** This is highly relevant and has a strong before-and-after metric and pilot scale. Clarify how “time to first payment” was calculated and whether the result covers the pilot cohort throughout or only a subset.
- **Checkout-conversion bullet:** Strong quantified product evidence. Keep the unit of change precise: 61% to 66% is a **five-percentage-point** increase, not a 5% increase. Clarify what “shipping it to all merchants” covers and whether the measured lift was sustained after rollout.
- **RICE bullet:** Revisit the description of how you scored Reach. Estimating tickets each feature would close may describe expected support-ticket reduction more than the conventional meaning of Reach. A product reviewer may question the prioritization method unless the scoring is explained accurately.
- **Merchant-interviews and roadmap bullet:** This contains useful discovery and business-case work, but the sequence of activities obscures what decision you influenced. Clarify your role in setting the priority and distinguish your recommendation from the final decision-maker’s authority.
- **Checkout A/B-test bullet:** This overlaps substantially with the earlier conversion bullet. Keep it only if it adds a distinct, important detail about experimental design or analysis that isn’t already clear elsewhere. Be exact about what was randomized and what the reported outcome measures: session-level assignment and customer conversion are related but not the same unit.
- **Leadership presentation and rollout funding bullet:** This supplies a meaningful stakeholder and follow-on result. Clarify whether funding was approved for a plan or whether the rollout to the additional regions actually happened; don’t let a reader confuse approval with deployment.

### Campus Food Rescue App

- **Launch and food-redistribution bullet:** It has useful scale and impact. Clarify whether 3,100 means students reached, registered, or eligible to use the app. Also make sure the 9-tonne figure reflects measured redistribution and that the basis for describing the food as otherwise wasted is supportable.
- **Weekly-active-user bullet:** Correct the stated percentage or one of the user counts: going from 400 to 1,150 is a **187.5% increase**, not 150%. Also clarify the time period over which the increase occurred and whether both figures use the same WAU definition.
- **Pickup-slot fill-rate bullet:** Going from 75% to 95% is a **20-percentage-point** increase, not a 20% increase. Clarify the measurement period and whether the change was observed across both halls consistently.

### MBA Consulting Practicum

- **Savings estimate and funded-pilot bullet:** Clearly distinguish a modeled annual saving from realized savings. The pilot funding is a useful outcome, but it is not evidence that the full projected saving was achieved.
- **Patient-intake bullet:** The passive construction hides what you did. Clarify your role, the actual change adopted, and its effect—such as intake time, completion, or staff workload—if you measured one.
- **COO presentation and pilot-approval bullet:** This appears to repeat the approval outcome in the first practicum bullet. Combine or differentiate the bullets so one establishes the analysis and the other adds a genuinely separate contribution; avoid spending space on the same approval twice.

### Skills

- **Product skills:** The list is relevant, but it names methods rather than showing depth or scope. Make sure the strongest product capabilities evidenced in your experience are represented, and include additional skills only if you can discuss them confidently.
- **Tools:** Jira, Amplitude, and Excel are useful. The list is short, and it does not indicate what you used each tool for. Add only tools you have actually used and that are relevant to the roles you’re targeting; don’t add SQL or other tools simply because they commonly appear in PM postings.

## Actionable improvements

### Tier 1 — high impact

1. **Make your target role and career direction unmistakable.** The résumé currently jumps from an MBA and product internship to a current mechanic role without explaining the direction. This is the first issue to resolve for recruiter confidence.
2. **Fix the food-app growth percentage and percentage-point wording.** The WAU figures conflict with the stated increase; the fill-rate change is expressed in ambiguous units. These are visible credibility issues.
3. **Remove or differentiate repeated outcomes.** The two checkout bullets both claim the conversion result, and the two practicum bullets both mention pilot approval. Duplication makes the résumé look less substantial than the underlying work may be.
4. **Clarify the RICE Reach method and A/B-test measurement.** Product interviewers may probe both. Accurate definitions matter more than using familiar product terminology.
5. **Separate projected results from achieved results.** In particular, make the consulting savings estimate and any pilot approval distinct from realized savings or implementation.

### Tier 2 — medium impact

1. Strengthen the carrier-scorecard bullet with the decisions or outcomes it enabled.
2. Add measurement context where useful: time period, baseline, definition, or how an estimate was calculated.
3. Clarify what “all merchants,” 3,100 students, and the 9-tonne figure represent.
4. Reassess routine maintenance and task-list bullets for whether they demonstrate relevant, distinctive evidence.
5. Expand the skills section only with real, defensible capabilities that the experience supports.

### Tier 3 — cosmetic or dependent on the final format

1. Consider whether education locations are worth the space.
2. Keep date formats consistent and check page layout after formatting. Visual balance, page count, and line breaks can’t be evaluated from the plain-text version.

**Verdict:** Address the Tier 1 items first. Tier 2 improvements are useful where you have reliable details; cosmetic adjustments should come last.
