## Overall assessment

You have credible product evidence: measurable onboarding and conversion results, user research, roadmap work, and a product internship in payments. The main problem is how that evidence is presented. The résumé doesn’t clearly establish what role you want, puts your most relevant product experience below older operations experience, and repeats the same checkout experiment in two bullets. Your current mechanic role also makes the career direction less obvious, so the résumé needs to make your intended path clear rather than leaving the reader to infer it.

## Changes to make, by section and bullet

### Header and structure

- **Make the target role clear near the top.** There’s no summary or other signal that tells a recruiter you’re applying for product roles. This matters especially because your current job title points in a different direction.
- **Reorder experience in reverse chronological order.** The Harbor Payments internship from 2024 should appear before Crestline Logistics, which ended in 2023. The current order makes an older, less directly relevant role more prominent.
- **Decide how to present the mechanic role for product applications.** Keep it accurate, but make sure it doesn’t unintentionally suggest that you’re no longer pursuing product. If applying to operations roles, the role may instead be a direct strength.
- **Use a real, professional contact address and a working profile link if the supplied example details are placeholders.** Placeholder-style contact details can undermine credibility if they appear in an actual application.

### Education

- **Keep the MBA first.** It is the most relevant credential for a product or business role.
- **Check whether the MBA date should indicate completion or expected completion.** Use the accurate status so there’s no ambiguity about whether the degree has been awarded.
- **Consider whether education needs to take as much space as experience.** Your strongest evidence for product roles is in the internship and project results, not the degree dates.

### Coastal Marine Services — Diesel Mechanic

- **First bullet:** Preserve the scope and turnaround result, but make your individual role and how the five-day measure is defined easy to understand. A product recruiter may otherwise see it as disconnected technical detail rather than evidence of execution and operational ownership.
- **Second bullet:** Clarify the scale and significance of the inspection work if you can substantiate it. As written, it describes maintenance and recordkeeping but gives little indication of the outcome or level of responsibility.

### Crestline Logistics — Operations Analyst

- **“Owned the weekly carrier scorecards…”** Strengthen the evidence of what changed because of your work. The meeting description is weak on its own: it says the meeting discussed the scorecards, but not what decisions or results followed.
- **“Cut warehouse pick errors 30%…”** Keep this achievement prominent. Clarify the measurement period or baseline if available, and distinguish your contribution from the supervisors’ and pickers’ work.
- **“Ran the quarterly S&OP review…”** This is a strong bullet with a clear result. Make sure the meaning and calculation of forecast error are defensible, and show your specific role in establishing the consensus forecast.
- **“Led the rollout of a route-planning tool…”** This combines rollout, process documentation, driver training, vendor negotiation, and savings in one bullet. Separate or prioritize the elements so the reader can quickly understand your ownership and how the 1,800-hour figure was measured. It is also a long bullet competing with your product experience for attention.

### Harbor Payments — Associate Product Manager Intern

- **“Redesigned merchant onboarding…”** This is a strong product result. Clarify what the verification change involved and how the pilot population and median time were measured, so the result is credible and understandable.
- **“Lifted checkout conversion…”** This is also strong, but it appears to describe the same A/B test as the later bullet about session randomization. Consolidate the experiment evidence so the results aren’t repeated.
- **“Prioritized the dispute roadmap with RICE…”** Explain the ticket-based Reach measure sufficiently for a reader to understand it. RICE Reach usually needs a defined population and timeframe; support-ticket volume may be a proxy, but its use should be clear. Also show what your prioritization informed.
- **“After interviewing 25 merchants…”** This overlaps with the preceding roadmap bullet. Clarify how the research and sizing affected the decision, and avoid presenting two bullets that seem to claim the same roadmap outcome.
- **“Ran the checkout A/B test…”** This repeats the conversion-test achievement above. Keep the most useful evidence about test design and results together, and remove the duplicated account of the same work. Be precise about what the five-point lift measures and why you describe its effect as being on customers.
- **“Presented the onboarding results…”** Keep this if you can show your role in securing the decision, but make the relationship between your presentation and the funded rollout precise. “Who funded” is a meaningful validation signal; the reader will want to know what was funded and whether you owned any subsequent rollout work.

### Projects

**Campus Food Rescue App**

- **“Launched…redistributing 9 tonnes…”** This is a strong scope and impact bullet. Clarify the timeframe behind the tonnage and your role in the launch if you were one of several contributors.
- **“Raised weekly active users…”** Retain the growth result, but be ready to define weekly active users and the before-and-after periods. The interview count supports the decision; make sure the causal link between the research and reminders is accurate.
- **“Raised the pickup-slot fill rate…”** The before-and-after figures are clear. Clarify the timeframe and whether the change is a 20-percentage-point increase, since the stated movement is from 75% to 95%. Also make your contribution to the volunteer system clear.

**MBA Consulting Practicum**

- **“Sized a $2.4M annual saving…”** This is valuable evidence, but distinguish a modeled opportunity from realized savings. The client funding a pilot is useful validation; it does not establish that the savings have already been achieved.
- **“Patient intake at 4 clinics was mapped…”** This passive construction obscures who did the work and what improved. Clarify your contribution and the result of shortening the form, if there was a measurable one.
- **“Presented the final recommendation…”** This appears to repeat the pilot approval already mentioned in the savings bullet. Keep the most informative account of the client decision and use the other bullet space for distinct work or impact.

### Skills

- **Expand the section only with skills you can substantiate and that match each job posting.** The current list is thin for product roles, particularly in analytics and technical collaboration. Don’t add tools or methods just to match keywords.
- **Clarify proficiency and context where relevant.** A bare tool name doesn’t show whether you used it for analysis, tracking, experimentation, or project coordination.
- **Use headings that support the roles you’re targeting.** “Product” and “Tools” are clear, but the section should reflect genuine strengths rather than serve as a keyword list.
