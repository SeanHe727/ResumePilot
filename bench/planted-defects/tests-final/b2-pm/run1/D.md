## Actionable improvements

### Tier 1 — high impact
1. **Correct the weekly-active-user percentage.** The stated starting value, ending value, and percentage cannot all be right. This is the clearest accuracy issue and could undermine trust in otherwise strong metrics.
2. **Make your target role and career direction clear near the top.** There is no summary or headline, while your current title points away from product. A concise positioning section should clarify the product roles you’re pursuing and connect the relevant parts of your background; it should not imply a formal PM history you don’t have.
3. **Reorder the experience entries into reverse chronological order.** The Harbor Payments internship from 2024 currently appears after Crestline, which ended in 2023. The current role should remain first, followed by the 2024 internship, then Crestline.
4. **Remove or distinguish the repeated checkout experiment.** The two bullets appear to claim the same test and conversion lift. Repetition takes space from other product contributions and can make the internship look thinner than it is.
5. **Clarify your contribution and the result in the hospital intake bullet.** Its passive construction makes it difficult to tell what you personally did or what changed.
6. **Address the recent career transition.** The sequence from operations to MBA/product internship to mechanic is unusual for a product application. Use a brief, factual explanation in the resume or application materials if it reflects a deliberate path; don’t leave the reader to guess.

### Tier 2 — medium impact
1. **Tighten the Crestline scorecard bullet.** It describes ownership of a recurring task but doesn’t say what the scorecards or review meetings changed. Add decision context or impact if you can substantiate it.
2. **Make individual ownership more visible.** Several bullets describe multi-step work or team outcomes. Ensure each makes clear what you personally decided, led, built, or delivered.
3. **Clarify the scale and scope of product rollouts.** In particular, make clear what “all merchants” refers to relative to the later regional rollout.
4. **Explain or accurately qualify the RICE method.** The detail about how Reach was scored may be useful, but it could also distract unless it reflects the method precisely.
5. **Make the skills section more specific where truthful.** Product skills are relevant, but the section offers limited evidence of technical analytics or software-delivery workflows. Add only capabilities you could discuss concretely in an interview.

### Tier 3 — cosmetic
1. **Consider whether the first diesel-mechanic bullet needs both engines and gearboxes.** Keep the detail that best supports your intended narrative; the mechanic experience itself is not a problem if it is presented honestly.
2. **Standardize the style of metrics and units.** Consistency will make the results easier to scan.
3. **Check the final rendered document.** This text version doesn’t reveal whether the resume fits cleanly on the page or has awkward line breaks.

**Verdict:** Address the Tier 1 accuracy, ordering, repetition, and positioning issues first. Tier 2 improvements are worthwhile where you have substantiating detail. Tier 3 edits are lower priority.
