# Resume review

You have strong, measurable results—especially in operations, forecasting, onboarding, and product experiments. The main issues are **the order of your experience, overlapping or repetitive bullets, a couple of metric inconsistencies, and an unclear career story**. I’m not rewriting any lines below; these are changes to make and why.

## Structural changes

- **Reorder Experience by date.** Put the Harbor Payments internship above Crestline Logistics. The current order isn’t reverse chronological, which can make your timeline harder to scan.
- **Move Skills higher if you’re targeting product roles.** Place it near the top, after Education or a brief summary, so recruiters can quickly see your product methods and tools.
- **Clarify your target role.** The resume combines product work, operations, an MBA, and a current diesel-mechanic role. If you’re applying for product or operations positions, consider adding a concise, targeted summary that makes the connection between your background and that role clear. If you’re targeting mechanical roles, the product experience should be positioned differently.
- **Check that the resume’s claims are supportable.** Be ready to explain the source, timeframe, and calculation behind every metric. Distinguish pilot results and projected savings from results achieved at full scale.
- **Keep formatting consistent.** The pasted version breaks several bullets across lines. In the document, make sure these are normal text wraps, not manual line breaks, and use consistent punctuation and spacing.

## Experience

### Coastal Marine Services — Diesel Mechanic

- **Engine and gearbox bullet:** Clarify what “under 5 days per job” refers to and whether that result is typical across the fleet or limited to particular jobs. The vessel count gives useful scope, but the time claim needs a clear basis. If applying for product roles, this bullet may be less relevant than your earlier product and operations results.
- **Hydraulic systems and service records bullet:** Add the scope or outcome if you can substantiate one—for example, the scale of the maintenance work or whether the records supported successful inspections. As written, it describes responsibilities without showing their impact.

### Harbor Payments — Associate Product Manager Intern

- **Merchant onboarding bullet:** Keep the pilot size and time-to-first-payment result, but clarify your specific contribution to the redesign. Also make clear that the measured result is from the pilot, rather than implying it applied to all merchants.

State the change as **five percentage points**, not just a five-percent increase, and include the experiment’s statistical confidence or significance if you have it. This bullet overlaps with the later A/B-test bullet, so avoid presenting the same experiment and result twice.
- **Dispute roadmap bullet:** Revisit the description of how Reach was scored. Counting support tickets a feature might close may not represent the number of users or accounts reached, so make sure the method is accurately named and described. If you have room, clarify what decision the prioritization informed.
- **Merchant interviews and roadmap bullet:** This lists several steps but doesn’t say what priority was chosen or what changed as a result. Clarify your ownership and outcome. It also overlaps with the dispute-roadmap bullet; make sure the two bullets describe distinct work.
- **A/B-test bullet:** This appears to repeat the checkout experiment in the earlier conversion bullet. Remove or substantially differentiate the duplicate. Also check the description of the outcome: randomizing by session and describing an effect on customers can leave the unit of analysis unclear. State the measured unit accurately and include significance if available.
- **Leadership presentation bullet:** Keep the rollout decision, but clarify whether the two additional regions were approved, funded, or actually launched. This helps distinguish a proposal’s approval from a completed rollout.

## Education and Skills

- **Education:** The dates and institutions are clear. If you’re applying now, ensure the MBA end date reflects whether the degree was completed. You don’t need to add GPA or coursework unless either strengthens your fit for a specific role.
- **Product skills:** Consider organizing these around the capabilities in your target job descriptions. “Requirements writing” is less standard phrasing than many ATS keyword lists use, so check the wording in relevant postings and use accurate matching terminology.
- **Tools:** Excel and Amplitude are useful, but the section is short. Add other relevant tools or technical capabilities only if you can confidently discuss them. For product roles, include skills that substantiate your work with experiments, analytics, prioritization, and roadmaps—not tools you’ve only briefly used.
