## Overall read

The main concern is **credibility and clarity**, not a lack of accomplishments. Resolve those before polishing wording.

## What to change, section by section

### Header and personal details

- **Remove your date of birth.** It is not needed for a typical résumé and can introduce irrelevant bias.
- **Remove nationality unless an application specifically asks for it.** If work authorization is relevant, address that directly and accurately in the application process.
- **Check the LinkedIn-style URL.** Make sure it leads to a complete, current profile; use a clean, professional link.
- **Consider adding a short role-positioning line or summary.** There’s no summary now, so a recruiter must infer your target from the experience. This is especially useful if you’re applying beyond payments. Keep it specific rather than generic.

### Education

- **Clarify the MBA’s status if you’re submitting this after June 2025.** The listed end date has passed; make sure the résumé indicates whether the degree was completed.
- **Consider including GPA or academic distinctions only if they’re strong and relevant.** Their absence is not inherently a problem.

### Harbor Payments — Associate Product Manager Intern

- **“Improved checkout conversion by 35%…”** Clarify whether 35% means relative growth or percentage points, and provide enough measurement context to make the result credible. “After replacing” implies causation; be prepared to support that with evidence.
- **“Wrote the requirements and success metrics…”** This is relevant product work, but it currently emphasizes process more than impact. Make the shipped feature’s result or purpose clearer if you have evidence for it. Also be precise about your role versus the team’s.
- **“Interviewed 25 merchants…”** Keep this: it shows discovery and data analysis. Clarify how the $1.1 million estimate was calculated and what period or population it covers, since it drove a roadmap decision.
- **“Ran weekly triage…”** The ticket volume and queue reduction are useful operational metrics. Clarify the scope of the queue and whether you personally closed tickets or coordinated their resolution.
- **“Measured the onboarding redesign…”** This currently describes an evaluation method but gives no finding. Add the result if you have it, or remove it if it does not add evidence beyond the later onboarding bullet. Also note that merchants who opted in may differ from those who did not, so this comparison alone may not establish causation.
- **“Redesigned merchant onboarding…”** This is one of your strongest bullets: it gives scope, a before-and-after outcome, and a pilot. Put the strongest relevant evidence early in the role. Make sure the pilot result is not presented as applying to all merchants.
- **Review the order and overlap of the six bullets.** The checkout, onboarding, and measurement bullets need to read as distinct accomplishments, not repeated descriptions of the same work.

### Crestline Logistics — Operations Analyst

- **“Cut late deliveries from 11% to 7% by excluding weather-delayed shipments…”** This is a serious credibility problem. Excluding delayed shipments can make the reported rate look better without improving actual delivery performance. Don’t present a changed calculation as an operational improvement. Use a consistent, transparent definition and distinguish measurement changes from real performance changes.
- **“Cut warehouse pick errors 30%…”** Strong operational evidence. Clarify the measurement period or comparison basis if available; keep the collaboration and training details that show how the result was achieved.
- **“Coordinated the quarterly S&OP review… and prepares…”** The tense shifts from past to present. Make the timing consistent. Also, the bullet mainly lists responsibilities; include its outcome or decision impact if you have one.
- **“Led the rollout of a route-planning tool…”** Good scope and adoption evidence. Clarify whether the 1,800 hours are measured savings or an estimate, and over what period the annual figure applies.

### Campus Food Rescue App — Product Lead

- **“Launched…to 3,100 students…”** Distinguish how many students had access from how many actually used the app. The 9-tonne result is compelling; make sure the timeframe and how the food was counted are clear.
- **“Launched pickup reminders…”** This shows customer discovery followed by product action and adoption growth. Clarify what “weekly active users” means and whether the comparison covers equivalent periods.
- **“Set up a volunteer shift system…”** Useful operational result. Make clear what the 95% rate measures and whether the change in staff cover shifts occurred over the same period.

### MBA Consulting Practicum — Regional Hospital Network

- **“Held weekly working sessions…”** This is a responsibility, not yet an accomplishment. Add the project’s outcome or your contribution to a decision if you have evidence.
- **“Patient intake…was mapped…”** The passive voice obscures your role, and “adopted” does not show the effect of the change. Clarify your contribution and whether the shortened form improved a measurable aspect of intake.
- **“Halved the time it takes a new merchant to receive a first payment…”** This does not fit a hospital scheduling project and closely repeats your Harbor onboarding achievement. **Remove it from this project unless you can establish that it is accurate and belongs here.** Replace the space with a genuine hospital-project outcome if one exists. As written, this is the résumé’s most damaging inconsistency.

### Skills

- **Expand the evidence behind the skills list selectively.** The listed methods and tools are relevant, but the section is thin compared with your claimed product work. Include only additional skills you can substantiate—don’t add terms solely to attract ATS matches.
- **Make the skill groupings reflect your target roles.** At present, the categories are broad, and the list doesn’t show the depth of your analytics or experimentation experience.
- **Don’t list a method as a skill unless you can explain how you used it.** In particular, be ready to discuss what your A/B testing experience involved, since one of the measurement bullets describes a non-randomized opt-in comparison.
