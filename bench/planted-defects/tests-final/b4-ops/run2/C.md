Your strongest material is the quantified supply-chain and warehouse work. The main fixes are two technical inconsistencies, one incorrect percentage, and a project bullet that appears to duplicate a job accomplishment. Here’s what I’d change, in resume order—without rewriting the lines.

### Header and education
- **Date of birth and nationality:** Remove both for a typical U.S. job application. They do not help establish your qualifications and can introduce bias. If a role asks about work authorization, address that separately.
- **Contact information:** Make sure the profile URL leads to a complete, current profile. Otherwise, the header is clear.
- **Education:** No substantive change needed. Check that the school and degree names match their official names.

### Crestway Foods
- **Inbound freight cost:** Keep the before-and-after figures and the milk-run explanation. This is a strong, credible result.
- **Safety stock / EOQ:** Correct the method. EOQ determines an order quantity by balancing ordering and holding costs; it does **not**, by itself, set safety stock. State the method you actually used to set safety stock and the outcome, if you can substantiate one.
- **Supplier scorecard:** Clarify your contribution to the renegotiations and, if available, their result. As written, the bullet shows adoption by buyers but stops before the business impact.
- **Sales and operations planning:** Add a concrete decision or result that came out of the meeting, if available. Otherwise, this reads mainly as a responsibility.
- **Warehouse lead time:** Recheck the claim that lead time halved while throughput stayed constant **and** work-in-process doubled. Those measures conflict under Little’s Law when measured over the same process and period. Fix the measurement scope or the figures before using this bullet.
- **Demand planning:** Keep this prominent; it is one of your best bullets. Be ready to support how stockouts and inventory were measured.

### Lakeview Distribution
- **Pick paths:** Keep. It connects your action to two useful operating measures.
- **Returns desk:** Keep; the time-study-to-triage-to-result chain is clear.
- **Dock scheduling:** Keep, but check whether “trailer wait at the dock” accurately describes the interval measured.
- **Cycle counting:** Separate or remove the unrelated night-shift, training, hiring, and certification duties. They obscure the cycle-counting work and make it unclear what caused the accuracy improvement.

### Projects
- **Last-mile routing, first bullet:** Remove it. It uses broad claims without evidence and repeats the substance of the following, stronger bullets.
- **Last-mile routing, route-length bullet:** Correct the percentage: 120 miles to 90 miles is a **25%** reduction, not 40%. Clarify whether this was a modeled result or one observed during the pilot.
- **Last-mile routing, pilot bullet:** Keep the pilot detail. Clarify your relationship with the grocer if needed, since “Independent Project” may otherwise suggest a self-directed exercise with no external participant.
- **Food bank, re-slotting:** Keep; the action and result are specific.
- **Food bank, donation intake:** Keep. Confirm that the one-day improvement was measured consistently.

Unless this was genuinely separate work with independently supportable figures, it undermines the credibility of both entries.

### Skills
- **Methods:** Correct the typo in “demand forcasting.”
- **Tools and methods:** Keep only skills you can discuss or demonstrate. Consider whether the listed tools are specific enough for the roles you’re targeting; the experience bullets should substantiate any added skill.

**Before applying, prioritize the EOQ claim, the lead-time/WIP figures, the routing percentage, and the duplicated forecasting bullet.** Those are more consequential than formatting changes because a hiring manager may question the underlying analysis.
