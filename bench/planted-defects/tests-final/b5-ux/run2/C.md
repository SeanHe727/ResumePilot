Your strongest material is the measured research impact. I’d focus on correcting two metrics, tightening claims about what the research proved, and making your own contribution clear.

### Header and education
- **Date of birth and nationality:** Remove them for a US-focused résumé; they generally aren’t relevant to hiring. If work authorization matters, address it only where appropriate and accurately.
- **Education:** Consider putting experience before education. Your recent work is now the stronger qualification.

### Northwind Health
- **Monthly survey/dashboard:** Specify the scope or a decision the care teams made using this work. “Owned” establishes responsibility but not its value.
- **Appointment reminders:** Clarify how the drop from 14% to 9% was measured, if you have that information, and distinguish your research contribution from the team’s decision to build the feature.
- **Screener and consent templates:** Say whether the two-weeks-to-three-days change reflects a measured typical setup time. The result is compelling if defensible.
- **Telehealth screens:** Clarify the period or comparison behind the 30% reduction in support calls so the outcome is credible.

### Pinegrove Financial
- **Onboarding:** Strong bullet. Confirm that the 41% and 58% figures use comparable definitions and populations; a before-and-after result alone doesn’t establish that the design caused the entire increase.
- **Dispute form:** Make your research role clear. As written, it sounds as though you personally designed and implemented the form. Also specify whether 40% is a relative increase or a percentage-point change.
- **Quarterly usability sessions:** Clarify what you did with the findings. The panel size and session count show scale, but the claim that *every* major release was tested needs to be supportable.
- **Statements research:** Fix the opening grammatical construction. Separate your synthesis work from the 23% call reduction, and explain the intervening product or service change; the current line jumps from research methods to outcome.
- **System Usability Scale:** Correct this before applying. **SUS scores range from 0 to 100**, so 104 is impossible. Check the source data, calculation, and whether both rounds measured the same flow.
- **Navigation card sort:** An open card sort can inform grouping and labels, but by itself it doesn’t establish findability in a redesigned navigation. Narrow the claim or identify the findability test you also ran.
