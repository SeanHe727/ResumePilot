# Resume review

The strongest material is in your analyst roles: you have measurable outcomes, decision-making impact, and concrete modeling work. The main priorities are to correct two valuation/metric issues, remove the duplicated forecast bullet, and make several claims clearer and easier to verify. I’ll describe what to change and why, without rewriting your lines.

## Overall structure and presentation

- **Move Experience above Education.** With several years of relevant work experience, employers will usually want to see that evidence first.
- **Keep the current caddie role, but give it less space than your finance roles.** It explains your current employment, but its relevance to finance is limited. Use the space for a specific responsibility or result from the junior tournament, if you have one.
- **Make dates, currencies, and number formatting consistent.** For example, choose one date style and use it throughout; keep currency notation consistent; and avoid splitting bullets across lines in ways that make them hard to scan.
- **Consider adding a target-role headline or short summary only if it helps focus the application.** Tailor it to the roles you’re applying for; don’t use it to repeat your skills list.
- **Keep contact details simple.** Your phone, email, and LinkedIn are sufficient. For finance roles, GitHub is generally unnecessary unless you have a relevant analytical or coding portfolio.

## Education

- **Keep the degree and university, but consider adding your major focus, GPA, or relevant coursework only if they strengthen your candidacy.** These details are most useful if your academic record is strong or you’re applying to roles where your education is especially relevant.
- **Use the same location and date format as the rest of the resume.** This makes the document more consistent and easier to scan.

## Experience

### Lakeside Golf Club — Caddie

- **The “about 5 rounds a week” bullet:** Clarify whether this is typical year-round or seasonal, if that affects the figure. The current wording communicates workload, but not much that helps a finance employer assess your contribution.
- **The junior tournament bullet:** Add your specific role or scope if you can substantiate it—for example, what part of the event you handled or how much you coordinated. “Helped” doesn’t show what you were responsible for.

### Stonebridge Manufacturing — Financial Analyst

- **The close-efficiency bullet:** Define what “close efficiency” measures and what it improved from and to. A 40% improvement is compelling, but without a clear measure or baseline, a reader may not know whether you mean close duration, manual effort, or something else. Include the tools or process changes if relevant.
- **The capital-plan bullet:** Clarify whether the template was adopted across all 12 requests and whether it changed decision quality, turnaround time, or consistency. The current result is mainly that requests followed the same rules; that’s useful, but the operational or business effect would make it stronger.
- **The board-pack bullet:** Explain the significance of the 5% threshold and your level of ownership. As written, it’s unclear whether you wrote commentary for every variance above that threshold or only selected items, and whether the work informed any decisions.
- **The acquisition-valuation bullet:** Check the cash-flow method against the discount rate. Discounting free cash flow to equity normally calls for the cost of equity; WACC is normally used with free cash flow to the firm. Make sure the model and description are consistent. Also clarify the valuation conclusion or how the analysis was used, if you can share it.
- **The group EBITDA-margin bullet:** **Correct or remove this claim.** Adding the margins of three business units does not produce a consolidated group margin. Calculate the consolidated margin using total EBITDA divided by total revenue, or use another valid measure. As written, the calculation is not mathematically sound.
- **The 18-month cash-forecast bullet:** This is one of your strongest bullets because it connects forecast accuracy to a treasury action. Clarify how “forecast error” was measured and over what period, if space allows. Keep the scale and debt-repayment outcome.

### Crescent Bank — Credit Analyst

- **The loan-underwriting bullet:** Keep the volume and value, but make the default claim precise: state the observation period and, if available, the performance window for the full approved group. A zero-default result is notable, but readers need enough context to judge the sample and timeframe.
- **The declined-applications bullet:** Clarify the period covered and what “early defaults” means. Also make sure the wording doesn’t imply that changing the thresholds alone caused the approval increase unless you can support that attribution. The before-and-after approval rates are useful evidence.
- **The covenant-checklist bullet:** Change the verb tense because this role ended in December 2022. Also clarify whether you created, maintained, or introduced the checklist and over what period the missed tests fell from seven to one.
- **The spreading-template bullet:** Keep the time reduction, and specify what “each credit memo” refers to if needed—for example, the relevant memo type or typical workflow. The measured reduction is strong; naming the tools or process involved could add useful context.

## Projects

### Regional Utility Valuation

- **The DCF/comparable-company bullet:** Include the valuation date or clarify what “within 6% of the market price” compares. Proximity to market price doesn’t by itself establish that a valuation was accurate, so give the reader the basis for interpreting the comparison. Name the company or ticker if it is public and appropriate to disclose.
- **The sensitivity bullet:** Clarify that the discount-rate change is a one-percentage-point change, if that is what you mean. Also make sure the two share values correspond to clearly stated assumptions and are consistent with the model.
- **The investment-club bullet:** Clarify whether the club actually used the work in a pitch or whether it was only shared for consideration. If it was used, say what role the analysis played; if not, avoid implying adoption.

### Student Investment Fund

- **The FP&A bullet:** Check the accuracy of the description. “FP&A team” sounds like a corporate finance function, which may not fit a student investment fund. Make the organizational context clear, or remove the bullet if it mischaracterizes the work.
- **The performance-report bullet:** Keep the benchmark comparison and committee use, but clarify what you changed in the report or how it improved the committee’s analysis. The current bullet has a useful adoption signal but little detail about the work itself.
- **The cash-forecast bullet:** This appears to duplicate the Stonebridge forecast in scale, accuracy, timeframe, and debt outcome. **Remove it unless it was genuinely a separate project.** If it is separate, make that distinction clear; otherwise, the duplication can raise concerns about accuracy and make your experience look padded.

## Skills

- **Expand the Tools section only with tools you’ve actually used and can discuss.** If relevant to your target roles, include systems or software used in your analyst work, such as ERP, reporting, or data tools. Don’t add technologies just to increase keyword coverage.
- **Make the Methods section more specific where it reflects real experience.** Your current list is relevant but broad; include additional areas only if you can support them through the experience bullets or discuss them in an interview.
- **Keep the skills section compact and credible.** Avoid proficiency ratings unless the job application specifically asks for them.

**Top fixes before applying:** correct the EBITDA-margin calculation, resolve the FCFE/WACC mismatch, remove or distinguish the repeated cash-forecast claim, and clarify the outcomes and timeframes behind your strongest metrics.
