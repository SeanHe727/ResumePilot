4. **Add a brief product-focused summary.** There is no summary, so the reader has to infer your direction from the timeline. A short, factual summary would connect your product work, operations background, and MBA without obscuring your actual current role.
5. **Clarify what was modeled, tested, shipped, or realized.** Several bullets describe pilots, projected savings, or rollout decisions. Make the status and scope of each result unmistakable.

- Correct the weekly-active-user growth calculation and express the pickup-slot change in percentage points. **Why:** These are visible numerical inconsistencies that can undermine trust in otherwise strong metrics.
- Consolidate the duplicate Harbor A/B-test material and repeated hospital pilot outcome. **Why:** You have enough distinct evidence to use the space more effectively.
- Clarify your product-career direction near the top and make the current mechanic role’s place in the story understandable. **Why:** Recruiters should not have to infer whether you’re still pursuing product roles.
- Add a concise summary if it can truthfully connect your product work and operations experience. **Why:** It helps establish relevance before the reader reaches the internship.
- Separate modeled savings from realized results and clarify the scope of pilots and rollouts. **Why:** This improves precision and protects credibility.

### Medium impact

- Revisit the explanation of Reach in the RICE bullet and make your use of the framework defensible. **Why:** Product interviewers may probe the methodology.
- Clarify attribution and time period for the route-planning savings, warehouse improvement, and food-rescue outcomes. **Why:** Readers need to understand both your contribution and how results were measured.
- Reduce or deprioritize less relevant mechanic-role detail if space is tight, while keeping the employment history accurate. **Why:** Product evidence should be easier to find.
- Make skill claims more specific where appropriate and supported by experience. **Why:** Broad skills are harder to evaluate.

### Cosmetic

- Check the final layout for crowded lines and clear section hierarchy; pasted text isn’t enough to assess this.

## Interview topics to prepare for

- **Checkout experiment:** Your hypothesis, experimental design, analysis, and how you interpreted the conversion change.
- **Onboarding:** What changed in the verification step, what the pilot measured, and how the wider rollout differed.
- **Roadmap prioritization:** How you used merchant research, business impact, and prioritization criteria to choose the next-quarter work.
- **Food-rescue app:** How you measured activation and weekly use, why you introduced reminders, and how you verified the reported growth.
- **Operations work:** How the scorecards, forecasting process, and route-planning rollout translated into decisions and measurable outcomes.
- **Current role and transition:** Why you are pursuing product roles now and how your recent work fits into that direction.

**Bottom line:** Keep the strong quantified outcomes, but correct the math, remove repeated results, and make your product direction clear. Those changes matter more than adding additional keywords.
