## Overall assessment

You have credible product-adjacent experience and several strong, quantified outcomes. The biggest issues are not lack of achievement; they’re **credibility and focus**: one bullet describes a reporting-definition change as an operational improvement, a practicum bullet appears copied from a payments project, and several metrics need enough context to be trusted. The résumé also needs clearer prioritization for product roles.

- **Company context:** Unknown. I can’t responsibly assess company-specific vocabulary, priorities, or required qualifications without a company and job description.

## Review by résumé section and line

- **Date of birth and nationality:** Remove these unless an application specifically requires them. They are generally unnecessary for a résumé and disclose personal information without showing role-related qualifications.
- **Contact details:** Make sure the profile link is a working, professional page. I can’t verify the example-domain link from the text.

- **MBA and economics degree:** Both are relevant background, but the education section does not show product coursework, projects, or other directly relevant evidence beyond the projects listed later. Add such detail only if it is substantive and useful for the roles you’re targeting.

### Experience

**Crestline Logistics — Operations Analyst**

- **Late-delivery bullet:** This is the most serious credibility issue. It says late deliveries fell because weather-delayed shipments were excluded from the on-time calculation. That changes the measurement, not necessarily delivery performance, and may read as KPI manipulation. Don’t present it as an operational improvement unless actual delivery performance improved independently of the revised calculation.
- **Pick-error bullet:** The reduction and two-site rollout are strong. Clarify the basis for the 30% figure and make your contribution distinct from what supervisors and the 45 retrained pickers did; otherwise the scope and attribution are hard to assess.
- **S&OP bullet:** One of your strongest bullets: it shows sustained cross-functional work and a quantified forecasting outcome. Make sure the forecast-error comparison uses a consistent method and timeframe.
- **Route-planning bullet:** Strong evidence of implementation, training, and measurable impact. Be ready to substantiate how the annual driver-hours saving was calculated and whether it was realized or projected.
- **Ordering:** This role predates the Harbor internship, so the experience section should be ordered consistently by date, with the more recent role first.

**Harbor Payments — Associate Product Manager Intern**

- **Checkout-conversion bullet:** A 35% improvement is compelling but needs context to be credible: clarify whether that means relative change or percentage points, the measurement period, and the population. “All merchants” is a very broad scope; ensure it accurately describes the rollout and measurement.
- **Dispute self-service bullet:** Good evidence of requirements work and cross-functional alignment. Add or emphasize the result of shipping the feature if you can substantiate its impact; delivery ahead of plan alone says less about product value.
- **Merchant research and chargeback analysis bullet:** Strong discovery and business-case evidence. Specify the currency and, if possible, clarify whether the $1.1 million is an estimate, annualized figure, or measured loss. The current wording leaves that ambiguous.
- **Triage and onboarding-tickets bullet:** The ticket volume and queue reduction are useful, but this risks making the internship look like support operations rather than product work. Keep it only if it helps show ownership, and distinguish your contribution from the team’s.
- **Onboarding-measurement bullet:** This states a method but gives no result. Comparing people who opted in with those who didn’t can also produce selection bias, so it does not by itself establish that the redesign caused an outcome. Explain what you learned or measured, and do not present this as an A/B test unless assignment was randomized.
- **Onboarding-redesign bullet:** Strongest direct product outcome in this role. Keep it prominent, but clarify how the pilot-region result was measured and avoid duplicating it elsewhere in the résumé.

## Changes to prioritize

### High impact

1. **Fix or remove the merchant-payment claim under the hospital practicum.** It appears duplicated from Harbor Payments and is the biggest trust issue.
2. **Reassess the late-delivery claim.** Excluding weather delays from a calculation is not the same as reducing late deliveries. Don’t claim an operational improvement unless you can show one.
3. **Add context to the 35% conversion result and other major metrics.** Specify the basis, timeframe, population, and whether the change was relative or in percentage points, as applicable.
4. **Resolve the A/B-testing mismatch.** The opt-in comparison described is not sufficient evidence of a randomized A/B test. Keep the skill only if you have separate, genuine A/B-testing experience.
5. **Improve the consulting bullets.** They currently emphasize meetings and vague or passive descriptions rather than your contribution and the hospital-related outcome.
6. **Reorder experience consistently by date.** Harbor should precede the older Crestline role.

### Medium impact

1. **Remove date of birth and nationality** unless specifically required for an application.
2. **Clarify how major estimates were calculated**, especially annual hours saved, forecast error, food tonnage, and user growth.
3. **Make the product focus more immediate.** A concise summary can help, but only if it adds a clear and accurate connection between your operations experience and product work.
4. **Review bullet priority.** Put the most relevant, defensible product outcomes first; reduce space given to support or activity-only work where it does not strengthen the application.
5. **Check the end date of the MBA** and ensure it accurately reflects whether the degree is completed.

### Cosmetic / lower priority

1. Remove or consolidate bullets that describe a method without a result, once the stronger evidence is established.
2. Check that units, time periods, and metric formatting are consistent.
3. Review visual hierarchy and length in the actual file; pasted text is insufficient to assess layout.

**Verdict:** Fix the high-impact credibility and consistency issues first. Medium-priority changes will sharpen the product story. Cosmetic edits are secondary.
