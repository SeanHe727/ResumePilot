# Interview bridge points

| Resume topic | What you should be ready to explain |
|---|---|
| Harbor onboarding | How you identified the onboarding problem, what you owned, how the verification change worked, and how you balanced speed with risk or compliance |
| Checkout experiment | The hypothesis, experiment design, population, metric definition, result validity, and how the result affected the product decision |
| Merchant interviews | How interview findings changed prioritization, not merely that interviews occurred |
| Route-planning rollout | How you drove adoption across depots, handled resistance, worked with the vendor, and measured the 1,800-hour benefit |
| Warehouse error reduction | How you diagnosed the root cause, selected the slotting change, trained users, and verified the 30% reduction |
| Food-rescue app | Your actual product ownership, how you prioritized features, and how you measured adoption and operational success |
| Hospital project | Which assumptions produced the $2.4 million estimate and how the pilot would validate or challenge the model |

## Bottom line

Your underlying experience is better than the current presentation suggests. The resume already contains credible evidence for early-career product work, especially in payments, experimentation, customer research, and operational execution. The most important changes are not stylistic: make the product direction explicit, correct the metrics, remove repeated evidence, clarify ownership, and ensure every major bullet communicates an outcome rather than merely an activity.
