## Structure and formatting changes

### 1. Use a targeted section order

For a product-focused resume, consider this order:

1. Header
2. Summary, if needed
3. Relevant experience
4. Selected projects
5. Additional experience
6. Education
7. Skills

For an operations-focused resume, keep Crestline Logistics near the top and treat Harbor Payments as relevant adjacent experience.

### 2. Resolve chronology

Your current order is not strictly chronological:

- Coastal Marine Services: Jul 2025–Present
- Crestline Logistics: Jul 2018–Aug 2023
- Harbor Payments: Jun 2024–Aug 2024

If you use a standard “Experience” section, recruiters generally expect reverse chronological order. Either:

- reorder all roles chronologically, or
- separate them into “Relevant Experience” and “Additional Experience” so the ordering is intentional.

### 3. Clarify the career narrative

The resume currently suggests this sequence:

- Economics degree
- Operations analyst
- MBA
- Product internship and projects
- Diesel mechanic

That may be accurate, but it needs explanation. A product recruiter may wonder why your current job is mechanical rather than product-related. A summary or cover letter can address the transition, but the resume itself should prioritize the experience most relevant to the job.

### 4. Standardize metrics

Be consistent about:

- percentage versus percentage-point changes,
- relative versus absolute increases,
- annual versus pilot-period results,
- modeled savings versus realized savings,
- users reached versus active users,
- company-wide rollout versus regional rollout.

The incorrect “150% increase” calculation is particularly important to fix.

### 5. Reduce responsibility-only bullets

The weakest bullets are those describing meetings, maintenance, mapping, or frameworks without outcomes. Aim for each bullet to communicate:

- what you changed,
- how large the scope was,
- what result followed,
- and your specific role.

Do not add metrics merely for appearance; use accurate scope and outcomes.

### 6. Check page length and density

The content can fit on one page only if formatted efficiently, but the Harbor Payments bullets are dense and overlapping. Prioritize the strongest outcomes rather than retaining every activity. For a product-focused version, the most valuable evidence is:

- onboarding improvement,
- checkout experimentation,
- merchant interviews and roadmap prioritization,
- leadership presentation and regional rollout,
- the food-rescue product metrics,
- selected logistics examples showing operational execution.

### 7. Add a summary only if it clarifies the transition

A summary could be useful if you are moving from operations and mechanical work toward product or strategy. It should clarify your target function and connect your experience, but do not use one simply to repeat the resume.
