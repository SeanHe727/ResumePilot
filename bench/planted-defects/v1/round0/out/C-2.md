## Overall assessment

You have strong technical content and unusually good metrics, especially in the Mobility Systems and Agent Runtime sections. The main problems are:

- Inconsistent chronology and verb tense
- Several bullets that are vague, duplicated, or not credible as written
- Excessively long, jargon-heavy bullets
- Metrics without enough context
- Uneven quality between older and newer experience

With editing, this could be a strong ML/agent-engineering resume.

## Highest-priority changes

### 1. Put experience in reverse chronological order

Mobility Systems Company should appear before Eastern Robotics:

1. Mobility Systems Company — Oct 2024–May 2025  
2. Eastern Robotics Co. — Sep 2023–Feb 2024

### 2. Remove or rewrite the credibility-damaging claims

Delete:

> Single-handedly built the company’s entire ML platform used by every team.

“Single-handedly” and “entire” sound exaggerated. Replace it with a specific, verifiable description:

> Built a shared ML platform for model deployment, evaluation, and monitoring, adopted by [X] teams and supporting [Y] production workflows.

If you cannot name the platform components, users, or impact, omit the bullet.

Also soften attribution in:

> after launch, the overall system reduced its pending-case backlog by 68%.

Because you built one branch of a larger system, use:

> ...contributing to a 68% reduction in the system’s pending-case backlog after launch.

### 3. Delete duplicate and empty bullets

Delete this Mobility bullet because the previous bullet already says the same thing with metrics:

> Fine-tuned a domain adapter that raised claim correctness, citation quality and tool-use accuracy...

Delete this Research-Agent bullet:

> Leveraged cutting-edge AI synergies to drive innovation across the organization.

It is generic and provides no evidence.

### 4. Make older experience as specific as the newer work

These bullets are too vague:

- “Responsible for...”
- “I worked on...”
- “Improved model performance by 35%.”

Specify what you built, what metric changed, and how. For example:

> Reduced diagnostics API p95 latency by [X%], from [A] ms to [B] ms, by [method]; added [unit/integration] tests covering [scope].

> Improved [precision/recall/F1/latency] by 35% on [dataset or production workload] through [method].

“Model performance” could mean accuracy, latency, throughput, or cost, so name the metric.

### 5. Shorten dense bullets

Several bullets run four or more lines and contain multiple accomplishments. Aim for one main accomplishment per bullet and generally no more than two to three lines.

For example, change:

> Built an industrial-diagnostics branch of a larger inspection system, automating triage of 800+ sensor signals per case through ML-extracted feature schemas, evidence-grounded review, and explicit abstention on low-confidence findings; after launch, the overall system reduced its pending-case backlog by 68%.

To:

> Built an ML diagnostics workflow that triaged 800+ sensor signals per case using structured feature extraction, evidence-grounded review, and abstention on low-confidence findings.

> Contributed to a 68% reduction in the system’s pending-case backlog after launch.

## Most important content gap

Your resume often gives the result without explaining your contribution, or gives the contribution without defining the result. The strongest formula is:

> **Action + system/component + method + measurable outcome**

For example:

> Reduced API p95 latency by 42%, from 310 ms to 180 ms, by batching database reads and adding request-level caching.

That level of specificity will make the older experience match the quality of your newer agent-engineering work.
